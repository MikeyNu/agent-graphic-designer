## Commerce, editorial, and marketing

### R01. Nyakio: the product crosses the surface boundary

**Visible choices.** A pale mint payment area shares a rectangular composition with a darker green product panel. Fine underlines, the progress rail, and restrained text keep the form visually light. Three colored bottles with white caps form a tilted, roughly triangular group. Bottles and foliage extend beyond the dark panel's left edge, while faint contour-like lines occupy its quieter right side. The dark primary button echoes the product panel. A larger, faded product scene surrounds the presented screen.

**Aesthetic reading.** The boundary crossing makes the product feel physically present in the composition. The red and amber bottles provide concentrated warmth against the greens; white caps bridge the object and the luminous form area. Hard rectangular surfaces and organic foliage make each other more noticeable. The outer background contributes to the presentation, but it is not necessarily part of the checkout itself.

**Transfer.** Compose product scale, silhouettes, overlap, and light together with the interface. Keep the form quiet without assuming commerce must be visually sterile. Preserve the material richness when improving details.

**Production questions.** The heading names one product while the image shows several bottles; clarify the purchased item or set. Persistent field labels, total/currency, and whether the final control proceeds to review or commits payment need an explicit implementation. The screenshot does not settle those semantics.

## Cross-example lessons

1. **The image can be part of the layout's geometry.** Boundary crossing in R01 and R12, the central cup in R02, and the shaped panels in R13 and R17 show distinct ways to develop that relationship. They are not interchangeable effects to add everywhere.
2. **Personality can live at different scales.** R06 uses huge word masses; R16 uses one altered image band; R20 uses a tiny identity mark and consistent spacing. Do not make every product equally loud.
3. **A coherent system can support variety.** Compare R03's interiors, R09's rows and portraits, and R10's varied data groups. Consistent vocabulary need not mean identical modules.
4. **Task convention and visual invention coexist.** These references repeatedly retain ordinary forms, lists, selected tabs, and navigation. Avoiding generic art direction does not require relearning every action.
5. **The selected styles are deliberately plural.** R04's square olive structure, R07's airy cream canvas, and R10's multicolor playfulness should not all become the same dashboard after adaptation.
6. **Aesthetic approval and production correctness are separate.** Preserve the pleasing material, rhythm, and character while repairing the specific content or behavior problem. Do not copy a source defect, and do not use that defect to strip the design's appeal.
