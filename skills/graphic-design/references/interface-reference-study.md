# Studying interface references without copying or overclaiming

Research snapshot: **30 October 2026**. Use this method for future research as well as the examples below. “Best” is contextual: curation, popularity, and attractive screenshots do not establish task success, accessibility, or conversion.

## 1. Choose the evidence for the question

| Source | Use it to investigate | Do not assume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Dribbble](https://dribbble.com/) | Art direction, composition, type, imagery, and visual alternatives in designer-published work. | A shot is a shipped product, a complete flow, responsive, accessible, or independently validated. |
| [Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/) | Real product screens and available sequences for particular tasks and platforms. | The captured version is current, every state is shown, or a successful company proves every design choice is good. |
| [Page Flows](https://pageflows.com/) | Order of actions, intermediate screens, feedback, and recovery when the actual recording is available. | An annotation list or locked thumbnail proves unseen behavior, errors, accessibility, or the present live product. |
| [Recent websites](https://recent.design/websites) | Distinctive web compositions, pacing, visual relationships, and links to original sites. | A preview recording proves live performance, mobile quality, keyboard behavior, or usability. |
| Original products, design systems, and standards | Verify behavior where access permits; establish technical requirements and platform conventions. | Marketing claims are controlled outcome studies or one system's visual theme fits every product. |

For substantial work, begin with a small set of independent examples: direct task peers, an adjacent product, and a visual reference outside the category. Include both desktop and mobile evidence where relevant. Search by the task or failure state, such as bulk edit, booking conflict, no search results, or account recovery, not only “beautiful dashboard.” Scale effort to the decision; do not make every minor edit browse four websites.

Inspect at least one example that challenges the first attractive direction. Avoid selecting only matching black-and-white SaaS pages or repeatedly sampling one studio. Seek relevant local language, device, bandwidth, and audience context rather than assuming a design feed represents everyone.

## 2. Inspect and decompose

For each useful reference, record:

| Field | Required distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Identity | Product, creator if known, exact page, platform, and captured version. |
| Dates | Original launch if verified, capture/publication date, and retrieval date separately. |
| Access | Live interaction, recording, screenshot, thumbnail, text-only, or inaccessible. |
| Observation | What is actually visible or reproducible. |
| Mechanism | The relationship producing the effect, expressed in plain language. |
| Transfer | What could help the current task and how it would change for this product. |
| Tradeoff | What might fail with different content, input, scale, or audience. |
| Rights and uncertainty | Assets or distinctive arrangements that must not be copied; untested claims. |

Study content order, grouping, hierarchy, typography, density, image function, navigation, feedback, transitions, and small-screen behavior. For a flow, note what information is requested when, what can be skipped, how a user backs out, and how completion is confirmed. If the recording omits errors, mark recovery unknown rather than inventing a favorable interpretation.

View actual images before making visual claims. A text extractor's image label is not visual inspection. When a page is blocked, use accessible public material and accurately label its limits. Do not bypass sign-in, payment, or verification controls, claim a subscription was available, or ask for credentials in a design brief. User-supplied licensed screenshots can support later work without implying unseen flows were reviewed.

## 3. Observed examples and access limits

The following is a bounded research sample, not a ranking or a comprehensive audit of these libraries. Observations distinguish browser-rendered previews from retrieved text. None of these sites was tested with users in this review. No third-party screenshots or assets are redistributed with this package.

### Recent: Shopify Design

- **Source:** [Recent's Shopify Design entry](https://recent.design/i/7yio1ee-shopify-design), linking to [Shopify Design](https://shopify.design/).
- **Inspected:** Browser-rendered gallery and enlarged recording preview. The entry displayed a relative posting age; the original launch date was not verified.
- **Observed:** Large black type dominates the opening preview. Later preview frames combine oversized numerals with commerce-related objects and use changing typographic placement for locations.
- **Transferable inference:** Let a subject provide both the visual material and the page's rhythm. A design-culture story can use type and imagery as a coordinated sequence.
- **Limits:** Live navigation, performance, mobile reflow, contrast, and reduced-motion behavior were not tested. Do not copy the lettering composition or assume large animated type suits a working dashboard.

### Recent: Becane Paris

- **Source:** [Recent's Becane Paris entry](https://recent.design/i/1s74lok-becane-paris), linking to [Becane Paris](https://becaneparis.com/).
- **Inspected:** Browser-rendered enlarged preview.
- **Observed:** A collection view presents a horizontal lineup of clothing silhouettes in a largely open field, with compact navigation and a collection heading.
- **Transferable inference:** Product range itself can organize the opening composition. The content can be the visual focus without a detached promotional illustration.
- **Limits:** The preview does not establish target sizes, selection affordance, mobile browsing, cart behavior, or the effectiveness of the sparse labels. A production adaptation needs those checks.

### Recent: Interfere

- **Source:** [Recent's Interfere entry](https://recent.design/i/mysyrbt-interfere), linking to [Interfere](https://interfere.com/).
- **Inspected:** Browser-rendered enlarged preview.
- **Observed:** Headline and supporting content occupy an upper band, with a broad issue-detail interface beneath. The product view shows a specific broken-link issue and structured diagnostic content; soft color surrounds the demonstration.
- **Transferable inference:** Demonstrate what a product helps someone understand or do. A gradient can remain secondary to specific information rather than becoming the entire concept.
- **Limits:** The product demonstration was not operated. Claims in the headline are not verified guarantees. Do not adopt this arrangement as the new compulsory SaaS hero.

### Page Flows: Etsy buying flow

- **Source:** [Etsy buying something on desktop web](https://pageflows.com/post/desktop-web/buying-something/etsy/).
- **Inspected:** Public page, a visible search/results preview, capture-version label, and annotation list. The page labels the version **February 2024** and lists 27 screens; much of the sequence requires access. The full recording was not reviewed end to end.
- **Observed:** The preview exposes product imagery alongside price and other comparison metadata. Public annotations enumerate search, item selection, option/personalization steps, delivery/payment details, and order-related steps.
- **Transferable inference:** Review commerce as a sequence of decisions, not just a polished product card. Preserve selected options and explain the current commitment at each step.
- **Limits:** The exact checkout behavior, error recovery, payment semantics, and current Etsy implementation remain unverified. Annotation order alone cannot prove them. Do not infer conversion performance.

### Dribbble: two project accounts

- **Sources:** [Business Twin by Eloqwnt](https://dribbble.com/shots/27850359-Business-Twin-Website) and [Credit Karma CRM by Jack R. for RonDesignLab](https://dribbble.com/shots/27845470-Credit-Karma-CRM-Credit-Monitoring-Dashboard).
- **Inspected:** Public project descriptions through web retrieval. Full-size visual inspection was blocked by browser human verification. These are **text-only research leads**, not inspected visual exemplars.
- **Reported by creators:** The first describes a business-decision simulator; the second describes reorganizing credit information around contributing factors. The latter includes outcome claims that were not independently verified.
- **Transferable hypothesis:** Let the user's decision or question determine the information hierarchy. Verify the actual UI and provenance before using either as visual evidence.

### Mobbin: public research surface

- **Sources:** [Mobbin](https://mobbin.com/) and its [MCP workflow article](https://mobbin.com/blog/how-to-use-mobbin-mcp).
- **Inspected:** Public text describing screen/flow research and examples of task-specific queries. The homepage rendered incompletely in the browser and the discover library returned Forbidden. The linked product screenshots were not visually inspected.
- **Transferable workflow:** Research a named task and its states across several products; use the library to compare mechanisms rather than request a generic look. The provider's examples are descriptions of its workflow, not this review's observed product flows.
- **Limits:** No claim of paid-library access, complete Mobbin flow inspection, or independent validation of provider statistics. Revisit with authorized access or supplied screenshots when a later brief depends on a particular example.

## 4. Synthesize an independent design

Write a principle in product terms: what a person needs to recognize, compare, decide, or recover. Then choose content, hierarchy, and behavior for that need. Describe the source mechanism separately from the new implementation.

For example, studying a collection lineup might suggest that a visual catalog should lead with comparable products. It does not justify copying the fashion imagery, exact spacing, letterforms, navigation, or animation. The same principle might produce a dense parts selector, an accessible sample grid, or a different editorial collection depending on the brief.

Keep functional conventions consistent while varying the composition where meaning permits. Run three low-fidelity alternatives for substantial new work and the [whole-page checks](interface-originality.md#4-review-the-whole-page-and-system). Avoid taking one website's art direction, another's navigation, and a third's components without reconciling them into a coherent system.

## 5. Refresh responsibly

Refresh links, captured versions, platform guidance, and time-sensitive claims when a new project depends on them. A gallery's posting date is not the product launch date. A 2024 capture can teach a stable interaction lesson but cannot establish 2026 behavior.

Distinguish standards, empirical studies, practitioner evaluations, source observations, and this skill's original synthesis. The sourcebook's [UI and UX evidence](sourcebook.md#ui-and-ux-evidence) explains the narrower support and limits. Research coverage should be described honestly even when access is incomplete; continue useful design work using available evidence.
