# Turn an open brief into an intentional design

Use when the user asks for something beautiful, creative, striking, professional, or distinctive without specifying its visual direction. Apply across interfaces, identities, brand guidelines, campaigns, documents, graphics, and motion. Supply the missing design judgment as part of the requested work. Do not require the user to act as an art director before you can begin.

This guide is design synthesis grounded in the owner's stated purpose and the bounded [research record](sourcebook.md#creative-direction-and-open-briefs). It is a working method, not a model of human consciousness or a guarantee of taste.

## Complete the brief without inventing the business

A short prompt delegates creative choices; it does not mean the user wants an average category template. Read the available project, supplied material, prior decisions, and relevant user preferences first. Separate what is known from what you can responsibly design:

| Missing information | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Composition, palette, typographic voice, imagery, icon style, pacing, or an appropriate hover response | Make and test a reversible design choice. Do not return a questionnaire asking the user to prescribe these. |
| Business offering, intended audience, product facts, prices, credentials, brand history, or approved claims | Recover from provided context; otherwise state a working assumption or ask the one consequential question. Never publish an assumption as fact. |
| Existing identity, supplied artwork, selected direction, or exact reconstruction | Preserve what is fixed; exercise judgment in the remaining space. |
| New functionality, external expense, publication, or a material business commitment | Respect the actual authorization. Creative initiative does not expand the business scope by itself. |

Do not infer price tier, geography, culture, gender, audience taste, heritage, or sustainability from a name alone. A name can suggest a visual exploration without establishing any of those facts. If a key answer is unavailable, continue with a clearly labeled proposal and complete unaffected work.

For a substantial new direction, form a compact working brief: the known subject and task, the experience worth creating, relevant clues, fixed constraints, and material assumptions. A few sentences suffice. Build from it rather than making paperwork the output.

## Form an idea from this subject

Before browsing finished designs, make provisional observations about the particular subject. Look at its name and language, actual offering, making process, materials, spatial qualities, people, setting, behavior, and supplied references. Include observations of the real product when available. Do not claim firsthand or cultural experience you do not have.

Find a promising relationship: precision with warmth, an expansive scene with a small quiet title, a repeated material detail with disciplined typography, or another contrast suggested by this brief. These are possible starting relationships, not prescribed aesthetics. Pure formal pleasure, a striking crop, or an intuitive arrangement can also begin the work.

Turn that starting point into a provisional intention: what should this feel like, and which visible relationship might make that happen? Test it through the three different roughs required for substantial new work. Do not protect the first idea because its explanation sounds clever. A name-derived pun is one option, not a requirement to depict the named object or repeat a metaphor everywhere.

A theme must affect actual choices. “Futuristic,” “luxury,” “editorial,” and “playful” are too broad by themselves. Specify the relationship among image, type, space, color, and movement that could express this particular interpretation. Avoid inventing a brand story to justify a style already selected.

## Use research to widen the idea

Research answers questions and supplies observations; it does not select the brand's identity by popularity. Replace a search for “best websites in this industry” as the entire creative method with focused questions about the subject, audience, material, typography, useful interaction conventions, or an unresolved compositional problem. Still inspect category work when it helps understand expectations and avoid duplication.

Study references for mechanisms and contrasts, including outside the immediate category. Record what you can transfer and what belongs to the source. Return to the subject after looking. If a reference or generated image has displaced every earlier possibility, deliberately try a different source, organizing relationship, or medium. Self-generated ideas can also become anchors.

Do not turn this into a ban on inspiration, history, research, or familiar patterns. Humans also use references and experience fixation. The research supports testing exploration methods within particular tasks; it does not establish that all models have one shared catalogue, that humans never converge, or that all AI-assisted work is uncreative.

## Design a coherent visual experience

Make choices together, then judge the visible whole. A collection of individually attractive components can still produce a generic or incoherent composition.

| Decision | What the art direction should resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Composition and density | What carries the experience, where attention rests, what information belongs together, and what can remain absent. Sparse and dense work are both valid. |
| Typography | How the actual words sound visually through proportion, weight, spacing, scale, and rhythm; verify glyphs, loading, and use size. |
| Color | Relationships, relative areas, contrast, image interaction, and intended character. An industry does not have one scientifically best palette. |
| Imagery | Subject, medium, viewpoint, silhouette, crop, lighting, and placement as part of the layout. Create appropriate assets proactively. |
| Iconography | Consistent operational meaning and optical family; expressive pictograms or illustration where the larger idea benefits. |
| Motion | What changes through time, what remains stable, and how pace, depth, or response belongs to the composition. A static treatment can be the strongest choice. |
| Repetition and variation | What makes the system recognizable, and how it adapts without making every page, size, or state identical. |

A scene, a very short title, minimal navigation, and one action can be complete when the image, spacing, crop, and meaning carry the experience. Do not add a badge, paragraph, second CTA, or feature grid merely to fill a presumed hero checklist. A richer composition can also succeed when its sections, copy, controls, and imagery have a clear rhythm. Do not force either density across every brief. Required information and working tasks must remain available.

Judge combinations in visible roughs before committing expensive finish. A stronger background may make most decoration unnecessary; expressive type may be the central asset; a utility page may need precise layout more than illustration. Choose tools after the idea suggests the medium. Follow [asset creation](asset-creation.md), [iconography](iconography.md), and [motion co-design](interface-motion.md) for execution.

## Make useful unrequested decisions

Within a creation request, take responsibility for details a designer would normally resolve: a considered crop, optical spacing, a useful illustrative asset, clear icon treatment, responsive recomposition, an appropriate interactive response, and annotations that make a handoff usable. Implement chosen improvements rather than listing them as optional future work.

Choose additions by their contribution to the actual experience, including beauty and delight. A subtle rotation may reinforce an object's dimensionality; restrained parallax may extend a spatial scene. Neither is required simply because the capability exists. Avoid automatic tilt on every card or compulsory scroll choreography. Keep controls stable, support touch and keyboard, provide appropriate reduced-motion behavior, and inspect the implemented sequence. Do not claim playback for a storyboard.

Extra care can also mean removing an unnecessary element, refining a single image, or leaving an effective quiet area alone. More components, effects, and generated assets are not evidence of greater creativity. For a narrow repair, supply the same care within the affected area and preserve the approved direction.

## Carry the idea into brand guidelines

Treat a brand guide as a designed explanation of an identity and how to use it. If an identity already exists, document it faithfully and distinguish proposed additions. If the request includes creating an identity, develop and test its visual language before codifying it. Keep unknown positioning and business claims provisional.

Adapt the scope to the actual brand and deliverable. Include the necessary identity assets, type hierarchy, color roles, image and illustration direction, iconography, layout relationships, and motion behavior where relevant. Demonstrate the rules in plausible applications, including difficult sizes or content. Show what changes and what remains recognizable.

Use annotations for decisions someone must reproduce: measure clear space from an identifiable feature; show the acceptable image crop or subject anchor; label color roles and valid text combinations; compare correct and incorrect use with the actual visible difference. Keep annotations readable and separate from the final artwork. Avoid arbitrary construction diagrams and decorative measurements that imply a process never performed.

Make the guide itself exemplify the identity through its page rhythm, hierarchy, images, and explanatory drawings. A set of hex swatches and font names is insufficient when the request calls for a usable system. An elaborate mockup is also insufficient without reliable source assets, specifications, and guidance. Render and inspect the actual document with the relevant format skill.

## Judge appeal and specificity separately

Review the artifact for craft, appeal, fit to the brief, and distinction. A polished result can still feel interchangeable. A novel result can still be unattractive or unusable. Do not use one judgment to excuse the other.

- **Replace the name mentally.** Which important choices would need to change for a different organization? If only the logo changes, inspect whether the direction has enough specificity. Quiet typography can carry identity; bespoke decoration is not mandatory.
- **Inspect the whole.** Do image, type, color, density, and motion feel like one intentional experience? Is the appeal present in the artifact, or only in the rationale?
- **Compare the alternatives.** Did the roughs explore different experiences, or merely different costumes for the same layout? Keep the strongest direction rather than averaging them.
- **Protect what works.** Identify the relationship giving the design its character before repairing defects. Do not flatten expression to satisfy a generic cleanliness checklist.
- **Test transfer when scope includes a system.** Apply it to another format, narrow viewport, dense page, or different state. Retain identity while changing composition appropriately.

These are qualitative prompts, not mathematical originality tests or proof of user approval. Do not declare a design unique merely because it has no obvious defects. Keep creative assumptions easy to revise when the user responds. The skill's responsibility is to produce a considered, inspectable result from an incomplete brief, then learn from feedback without reverting to a generic default.
