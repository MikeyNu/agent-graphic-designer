## Reports, learning, and assisted work

### R21: density with distinct emphasis

**Visible:** A slim icon rail sits beside an indented report tree. A broad pale canvas contains the large revenue figure, compact score tiles, avatar/value strips, platform rows, bars, a ranked list, and an expanded detail region. Pink recurs at very different scales: selected navigation, small value badges, a solid chart-side slab, and a faint detail tint. One black tile adds a separate tonal anchor. Fine hatching gives selected chart areas texture without saturating the entire report.

**Learn:** Density can have character through alignment, changing scale, and recurring small details. Keep compact substructures internally ordered while giving one figure or region priority. The expanded salesperson region stays adjacent to its ranked context instead of becoming another generic modal.

**Resolve independently:** Metric definitions, date scope, ranking logic, chart arithmetic, hover access, and expansion behavior are not established. The tiny labels need testing at actual delivery size. Do not import the source's platform marks or numbers as real product content.

### R22: three regions with different jobs

**Visible:** A narrow navigation column, wide task/performance column, and right profile/activity column share quiet white surfaces. The center has generous intervals between a greeting, a thin metric strip, a line chart, and task rows. The right column is denser and uses pale blue message/file blocks. Small pastel icon circles soften the otherwise restrained layout. A dark tooltip concentrates emphasis over two delicate chart lines.

**Learn:** Different densities can coexist when the regions have distinct roles. The conversation does not need to look like the chart, and the chart does not need a heavy enclosing card. Light separators and shared alignments make the whole feel related.

**Resolve independently:** Determine whose profile/activity is shown, how selection changes it, and which panel scrolls. Preserve the task and draft message when an activity item opens. Neither sending nor collaboration behavior is demonstrated by the image.

### R23: conversation beside a work artifact

**Visible:** A white metrics window appears behind an overlapping dark AI-assistant window in a presentation. The dark window separates history, conversation, and a spreadsheet-like product table. Thin cell rules, muted secondary text, and a small bright stop control keep most of the dark field quiet. The white overview uses green highlights within otherwise pale data displays.

**Learn:** An assistant's output can have its own persistent working surface alongside the conversation. Light and dark applications can share a density and alignment vocabulary without copying every surface value.

**Resolve independently:** The overlap is presentation evidence, not proof of a draggable in-app window or a route transition. The still does not show whether the table is editable, saved, generated completely, or connected to the source data. Specify draft, generating, canceled, partial, failed, and committed states before implementation. Some text remains limited by source resolution.

### R24: color repeated through geometry

**Visible:** A top navigation strip gives way to three unequal content columns. The left account card has a white face over a violet backing with a concave side detail. Violet returns in a bar and the lower chart panel; black, orange, and yellow provide other strong anchors. The arc, rounded bars, card contour, and pill controls form a curved visual family while financial rows remain simple.

**Learn:** Repetition across different objects can unify a page more effectively than identical cards. Sculpted geometry can accompany familiar financial controls. Treat the card as a composed object with edges and layers, not merely a colored rectangle.

**Resolve independently:** The navigation labels and financial content need a coherent product model. Define each arc/bar quantity and total before copying the visual encoding. The attractive silhouette does not establish mathematically consistent data or account-switch behavior.

### R25: an editorial learning sequence

**Visible:** Cream fields, fine rules, mostly square corners, yellow blocks, and purple details form a markedly different system from the rounded dashboards. Three numbered lesson covers combine large crops, sparse illustration, and fitted titles. Below, a vertical line connects square lesson icons with completion marks. A narrower schedule region and a yellow upgrade block repeat the same visual vocabulary.

**Learn:** A learning workspace can borrow the rhythm of a book or illustrated card collection. Use image scale, cropped continuation, connected steps, and confident color fields to give progress a physical presence. Keep the lesson text more conventional than the display heading.

**Resolve independently:** Cover continuation does not prove a carousel, drag gesture, or scroll animation. Completion, locked lessons, resume position, and schedule timezone require real definitions. Preserve the warmth and graphic restraint without copying its exact illustrations or words.

### R26: one luminous feature within a working surface

**Visible:** Translucent green insight cards overlap and diminish into depth above compact white analytics blocks. Green recurs in tiny bar segments, a logo-like mark, selected data points, and the assistant's identity. Black line work and fine vertical ticks balance the soft glowing stack. A full-height assistant region provides a different internal rhythm from the charts.

**Learn:** A single atmospheric feature can set the character while most operational content stays composed and clear. Depth, translucency, line density, and repeated small accents work together. Keep strong effects concentrated enough that they can be appreciated.

**Resolve independently:** The stack shows no verified swiping, rotation, parallax, or expansion. A motion proposal should expose complete insight text and a clear selection method. Metric definitions, reported time savings, chart sources, and assistant authority cannot be inferred from the design.

## Records, boards, and spatial operations

### R27: object context beside detailed work

**Visible:** A global sidebar leads into a separate person/activity panel, then a wider document workspace. The person remains prominent while the main area uses a breadcrumb, title, member group, tabs, count strip, and compact document rows. Most separation comes from fine gray lines. Bright contact actions and activity-state marks are isolated within this quiet structure.

**Learn:** Several information levels can be visible without turning every level into a contrasting card. Give object identity a stable home and distinguish global navigation, object sections, and document actions through placement and scale.

**Resolve independently:** Define what the approval/rejection icons do, their labels, access rules, and document version. Show preview, pending review, returned, and accepted states without equating a button click with an accepted document. Narrow screens need a deliberate route back to the same person and document list.

### R28 and R29: board repetition with one expanded object

**Visible:** Four pipeline columns use consistent outlined cards under a pale metric band. A dark, taller card exposes address, contact, owner, and date within one column. That reversal stands out strongly among quiet cards. Small count controls, metadata chips, avatars, and fine borders give the board a repeatable rhythm. R29 adds no independent evidence.

**Learn:** A repeated structure can support a distinctive selected or expanded object without losing the collection. Contrast and additional detail can carry emphasis; decorative variation on every card is unnecessary.

**Resolve independently:** The image does not prove whether dark means hover, selection, expansion, or another state. Define it. Board stage, selection, and pending movement are different facts. Specify move actions, non-drag pointer and keyboard alternatives, rejected moves, concurrency, and return position.

### R30: map, people, and event history as one workspace

**Visible:** A dark icon rail anchors a blue-gray driver list, pale map, and white activity panel. Coral and teal routes include stops and avatar markers. The map's low visual contrast makes the routes prominent. The activity column uses connected circular nodes with different fills and text status labels rather than many enclosing cards.

**Learn:** Keep distinct representations of the same operation spatially related. Route color, selected person, marker, and activity can form one identity system while the map remains a quiet field. Fine connectors and repeated circular geometry provide continuity.

**Resolve independently:** Marker location, route progress, delivery state, and data freshness need separate definitions. A visual route is not proof of live tracking. Do not silently recenter while the operator inspects another area; define follow mode, manual exploration, stale positions, and a list alternative.

### R31: a focused accent in a dense report

**Visible:** Pale bars surround one blue bar and its visible tooltip. The same blue appears in the primary proposal action, while four nearby summary metrics remain quiet. The lower table spans the work area and aligns identifiers, names, addresses, values, dates, and small status labels. Search/filter controls belong to the table's header region.

**Learn:** Dense operational pages can have strong hierarchy through one local accent and disciplined alignment. The table is a legitimate primary design object. Chart focus and action emphasis can be distinct even when they share a hue.

**Resolve independently:** A visible tooltip does not prove the trigger or keyboard behavior. Clarify metric period, selection, sorting, pagination, and full-address access. Restore scope when returning from a proposal, rather than rebuilding the overview from scratch.

### R32: different financial rhythms within a shared shell

**Visible:** A broad central income/expense chart has repeated bars; the right rail combines segmented savings progress and a payment agenda. Compact transactions sit below. Cyan, blue, purple, and green distinguish several data groups within fine-bordered white panels. Repeated tiny segments make the savings groups visually related without making them identical to the main chart.

**Learn:** Related financial content can use different internal structures: time comparison, goal progress, and scheduled items. Maintain shared labels, insets, numerals, and action placement while fitting each module to its content.

**Resolve independently:** The first goal shows 12,000 of 20,000 alongside 75%, although that fraction is 60%. This is a specific content contradiction, not a reason to reject the whole aesthetic. Derive the value, label, and filled proportion from one data definition. Avoid celebratory color semantics that conflate category, increase, and success.

## Settings as designed working surfaces

### R33: an orange thread through quiet rows

**Visible:** Orange connects the brand mark, active navigation icon, selected settings tab, switches, and Save action. Section labels and descriptions occupy a consistent left area; profile fields and controls align in the right area. Light rules and a pale work surface carry the structure. The profile picture is a small visual interruption rather than a separate feature card.

**Learn:** A settings page can feel specific through recurrence, alignment, and proportion. Keep long descriptions and small controls in predictable relationships instead of wrapping every row in a card.

**Resolve independently:** A global Save button and immediate-looking switches need an explicit persistence model. A security switch alone does not demonstrate completed security setup. Define draft, pending, configured, failed, and canceled states as appropriate.

### R34: dark settings with controlled surface steps

**Visible:** Near-black canvas, charcoal fields, medium-gray table header, and a lighter selected row create distinct levels. The payment form uses a left explanatory column and right fields; the contact-email section changes to radio choices; the billing history expands into a table. Blue links and pale status chips are small accents.

**Learn:** Dark interfaces need a deliberate value hierarchy, not one black background with white text. Reuse the section grid across different control types while allowing the history table its full width.

**Resolve independently:** Test delivered contrast and state differentiation. Do not transcribe the sample payment details into new work. Use the actual payment provider's supported fields and flow; the image does not define processing, persistence, or permissions. Separate selected invoice rows from settled payment status.

### R35: grouped preferences with a narrow second navigation

**Visible:** Main navigation, a small settings menu, and a wide preference area form three levels. Large white groups on a pale canvas contain label/description rows and right-aligned magenta switches. Magenta also marks the two navigation selections. The lower group continues beyond the frame.

**Learn:** Grouping and repetition can make a long preference list feel quiet and coherent. Accent color can tie selected location to active controls without requiring ornamental headings.

**Resolve independently:** Define whether each choice is personal, workspace-wide, or channel-specific. The screenshot's switch positions do not establish appropriate defaults or consent. Preserve discoverable pending/failure states and stable row positions when changes save.

### R36: spacious alignment within compact chrome

**Visible:** A very narrow icon rail and small horizontal tabs leave most space to a two-column form. Labels/help text remain aligned as control types change between fields, dropdowns, a color-swatch row, segmented date formats, and switches. A small connection-error mark sits beside its relevant label. Color is concentrated in the swatches and a few status details.

**Learn:** Consistent column starts and section spacing can unify many different controls. Leave enough space for guidance without artificially widening every input. Small status signals can remain local to the issue.

**Resolve independently:** The repeated Language & timezone heading over unrelated behavior switches is a copy/structure problem to repair locally. Connection errors need a useful explanation and recovery. Color choices need names and selected-state cues. Preserve unsaved values across tabs according to an explicit save policy.

### R37: a strong shell around a modest form

**Visible:** A black sidebar borders one rounded white settings plane. Compact tabs and fine rules subdivide profile, personal information, password, and email preferences. The form alternates paired fields and full-width fields. Blue switches are small against the mostly black/white composition. Global Save/Cancel coexist with a local Update password action.

**Learn:** An assertive outer frame can provide identity while the form itself stays conventional. Keep section labels, field widths, and actual input relationships intentional; equal-width inputs everywhere would lose some of this hierarchy.

**Resolve independently:** Explain the boundary between global profile saving and the separate password transaction. A role dropdown does not prove self-service role escalation is permitted. Specify authorization and unsaved-work handling from the real product contract.

### R38: a two-axis preference matrix

**Visible:** Nested navigation leads to event rows grouped under small purple category headings. Email, Desktop, and Mobile column headings repeat icons and a column-wide action. Purple switches align down each channel; thin horizontal rules maintain tracking across the wide field.

**Learn:** A matrix can expose relationships that would be buried in separate forms. Column alignment and disciplined empty space do substantial visual work. Do not automatically convert it into unrelated cards because cards seem more modern.

**Resolve independently:** Every control needs its event and channel identity. Define the scope and partial-failure behavior of a column-wide action. On narrow layouts, retain both axes through event groups with explicitly named channel controls or another tested arrangement. Off, unavailable, policy-controlled, and not saved are different states.

### R39: profile identity crossing into an editable form

**Visible:** A softly colored cover field has a large curved upper-left edge. The portrait crosses its lower boundary beside the Profile title and Save/Cancel actions. A smaller portrait appears again at the editable photo row. Quiet paired labels/fields continue below, with a small formatting toolbar in the bio area. The lower page is cropped.

**Learn:** Even a utility screen can include a composed identity moment. The shared portrait connects presentation and editing; the broad pale cover provides atmosphere without changing the familiar form structure.

**Resolve independently:** Define cover/photo editing, preview versus committed image, upload failure, cropping, and cancel behavior. Do not infer the page's complete length, every field, or live preview behavior from the crop.

### R40: variety across one restrained application

**Visible:** Six fragments share pale green surroundings, narrow navigation, small dark-green actions, and light working surfaces. They include dense records, a spacious assistant entry, a task collection, profile fields beside a preview, a transcript empty state, and records/settings content. The assistant composition is much more open than the task list, while the recurring shell and typography connect them.

**Learn:** Coherence does not require every screen to use the same content recipe. Let records compare, let an empty state explain an entry action, and let profile editing show an appropriate preview. A low-amplitude visual identity can still have warmth and variation.

**Resolve independently:** Tiny and cropped source text limits exact interpretation. No clinical validity, privacy controls, transcription accuracy, or AI authority was tested. In a new product, derive those contracts from its actual requirements. Keep evidence/proposals, editable drafts, and committed records distinct.

## Transfer into the next design

Use the new set to expand the design vocabulary: expressive learning covers, luminous stacks, dense analytical detail, selected board objects, spatial operations, and carefully aligned settings all belong. Do not assign a greeting, KPI row, hero, or card grid to every surface.

Design the active states alongside these compositions. Identify which object persists, what changes around it, and where a transition might add pleasure or continuity. The [motion co-design workflow](interface-motion.md) provides the shared plan; the later [nine-clip study](owner-motion-study.md) records temporal observations separately. Do not infer a complete animation recipe from a layered still.
