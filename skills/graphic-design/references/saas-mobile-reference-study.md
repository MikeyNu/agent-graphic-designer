# SaaS and mobile reference study

Retrieved **30 October 2026**. This targeted follow-up supplements the general [reference method](interface-reference-study.md). The seven project pages below were opened and their images enlarged in the browser. Original launch dates, production provenance, and outcomes were not independently verified. Approvly desktop and mobile are two related presentations by one creator, not independent evidence of a widespread pattern. No product actions were performed and no third-party images are distributed here.

Use these observations to form hypotheses, then validate the actual work. The resulting [SaaS guide](saas-product-design.md) and [mobile guide](mobile-app-design.md) are original design synthesis, not research conclusions that the depicted products have proven.

## CloudNexus: scoped diagnosis

- **Source:** [CloudNexus cost monitoring by Ibrahim Khalil](https://dribbble.com/shots/27850074-CloudNexus-Cost-Monitoring-Dashboard).
- **Inspected:** Three enlarged static images, including date selection and monitoring detail in presentation mockups. No operated filters or complete product recording.
- **Observed:** Workspace, project, and environment context accompany the selected service. Cost views expose date presets, explicit endpoints, and a UTC data cutoff. Monitoring panels ask diagnostic questions about latency, failures, load, and resources, with thresholds, units, comparison context, and incident annotations.
- **Transfer:** Carry scope through investigation. Organize charts around a user's question and connect events to the relevant time interval.
- **Critique and limits:** The visible partial-month and full-previous-month choices require a fair, explicit comparison basis. No normalization, linked filtering, permission enforcement, metric accuracy, or live freshness behavior was verified. Diagnostic proximity does not prove causation.

## Approvly desktop: evidence before action

- **Source:** [Spend approval dashboard by Dhrumil Patel](https://dribbble.com/shots/27843190-Approvly-Spend-Approval-Dashboard-for-Finance-Teams).
- **Inspected:** Three enlarged static images: overview, ordinary request detail, and over-policy exception. The creator labels the project a concept. The separate budget-forecast image was not inspected.
- **Observed:** A due-date queue shows requester, vendor, policy, amount, and next action. Detail retains a request ID, checks, approval chain, history, and a stated downstream consequence. The exception view exposes the limit, excess amount, required reason, and subsequent CFO step.
- **Transfer:** Make the decision evidence and commitment legible before action. Pair summary with inspectable records; design an exception path alongside the ordinary case.
- **Critique and limits:** A button labeled as an approval must accurately describe whether it completes this stage or the entire process. Claimed undo, queue updates, arithmetic completeness, and policy enforcement were not independently tested. Static panels do not establish focus restoration or concurrent-edit handling.

## Approvly mobile: a focused continuation

- **Source:** [Mobile spend approvals by Dhrumil Patel](https://dribbble.com/shots/27851266-Approvly-Fintech-Mobile-App-for-Spend-Approvals).
- **Inspected:** One enlarged three-phone storyboard from notification to review to confirmation. Other project images and actual native behavior were not reviewed.
- **Observed:** Review concentrates on one identified request, amount, rationale, budget impact, and policy status. Confirmation separates approved, scheduled, and expected-paid stages, then offers the next request. The notification preview includes personal and financial detail; the review action depicts biometric intent.
- **Transfer:** Adapt a desktop workflow into a focused mobile decision while preserving identity, context, and continuation. Keep future financial stages separate from completed ones.
- **Critique and limits:** Verify lock-screen privacy, current request state, authorization, biometric cancellation/fallback, uncertain outcomes, and genuine undo limits. The storyboard proves none of these, nor desktop synchronization, safe-area handling, or keyboard behavior.

## Mobile CRM: compact does not mean meaningful

- **Source:** [CRM dashboard by Ronas IT](https://dribbble.com/shots/27825094-CRM-Dashboard-B2B-SaaS-Mobile-App).
- **Inspected:** Two enlarged static presentations: onboarding/team view and team/performance views. The embedded animation was not reviewed end to end.
- **Observed:** Team identity, events, compact metric rows, period controls, and a separate performance view share a visual system. Large chrome forms dominate onboarding; event cards use related decorative icons. Member activity appears as percentage rings without a visible denominator in the inspected screens.
- **Transfer:** Shared rows can retain comparison in limited space; scoped detail can extend a summary without displaying all information at once.
- **Critique and limits:** Establish what activity and changes mean. Check whether horizontal event cards conceal urgent work and whether decoration delays useful content. Do not remove functional period controls because they are rounded. Navigation, gesture alternatives, chart access, and large-text behavior remain unverified.

## Eventrise: retrieval before presentation

- **Source:** [Eventrise attendee app by Kevin Bhagat for Heyo](https://dribbble.com/shots/27846360-Eventrise-Attendee-Mobile-App).
- **Inspected:** One enlarged three-phone image: event list, event/ticket detail, and ticket presentation.
- **Observed:** Upcoming/past grouping and event date/location lead to tickets for a selected event. The final view gives a QR code substantial space and exposes a wallet action. Multiple tickets are represented with pagination dots; back and close controls coexist in the presentation view.
- **Transfer:** Organize retrieval around recognizable event identity, then give the immediate access credential a focused presentation.
- **Critique and limits:** Specify which ticket is active, accessible switching beyond a gesture, and distinct Back versus close behavior. Offline validity, revocation, dynamic-code rules, scanning, wallet integration, privacy, and bright-light legibility were not tested. A wallet button or QR image does not prove a usable credential.

## Voice recorder: source-linked review

- **Source:** [AI voice recorder concept by Purrweb](https://dribbble.com/shots/27850542-AI-Voice-Recorder-Mobile-iOS-App-Design).
- **Inspected:** One enlarged phone presentation showing task review. Recording, library, transcript, and permission screens were not visible in the inspected image.
- **Observed:** Draft tasks and an open question include source timestamps. A readiness interaction precedes building a brief. The static image shows differing readiness counts in the heading and final action area.
- **Transfer:** Keep generated work connected to inspectable source material and a deliberate review step before commitment.
- **Critique and limits:** Reconcile readiness labels and provide a usable alternative if dragging is required. Tiny supporting copy needs actual-size review. Source playback, generated-content accuracy, cancellation, microphone access, persistence, and final action semantics remain unverified.

## Recipe app: discovery and use are different

- **Source:** [Recipe app by SM Rubel](https://dribbble.com/shots/27848879-Food-Recipe-App-UI-Design-Modern-Cooking-Recipe-Discovery).
- **Inspected:** One enlarged three-phone image with discovery, recipe detail, and favorites. A second image was not inspected.
- **Observed:** Search and categories support browsing; imagery, duration, serving information, ingredients, and a cooking action appear in detail. Favorites use a compact visual collection. Ingredient images lack readable quantities in this view, and the description names spaghetti while the main photograph appears to show a different pasta form.
- **Transfer:** Let visual recognition help discovery, then provide precise information and a focused path for performing the task.
- **Critique and limits:** Reconcile imagery and instructions; inspect labels, quantities, and the relationship between fixed controls and the last cooking step. Cooking mode, saved progress, search/filter behavior, serving changes, offline access, and accessibility were not operated. A rich food photograph cannot substitute for usable instructions.

## What this study changes

Treat a polished overview as the beginning of inspection. Follow the object into detail, an exception, completion, and return where those views exist. If they are absent, mark the behavior unknown. Do not extrapolate a complete flow from two neighboring phone mockups.

Use a paired desktop/mobile presentation to compare task priorities, not to certify responsive implementation. Cross-check identity, scope, amounts, counts, and state labels between screens. Preserve familiar useful controls while challenging ornamental repetition. For a future implementation, test the actual platform, current data, input conditions, interruptions, and return path before claiming that the adaptation succeeds.
