## Method and evidence

All nine files were inspected using one-second overview samples, followed by 26 denser windows containing 395 frame selections, generally 0.1 to 0.4 seconds apart. Every overview sheet and all 28 dense sheets were visually inspected. Seven additional crops examined source-resolution details of masks, text/image intersections, a button, a timeline, and the poster. Overview and dense selections can overlap; these are not counts of unique video frames.

**Access limit:** this was sampled-frame analysis. Continuous normal-speed playback, perceived smoothness, soundtrack, input latency, and the original live interfaces were not evaluated. Three files contain audio tracks, but their sound was not analyzed. No exact easing curve, spring constants, source DOM, animation library, shader, scroll pin, or performance is established by these frames. A presentation may include edited speed or compositing. A nominal 60 fps file does not establish a 60 fps production interface.

Times below locate observations within the recording. Ranges are approximate and bounded by sampling resolution, not measured source-code durations. A pointer near a changing object supports a possible interaction, not proof of event handlers. Read each section in three layers: **visible sequence**, **interpretation**, and **proposed construction**. The latter two are our judgments, not the creator's stated intentions. Study the supplied originals again when available; agents reading only this record must not claim they viewed them.

## C01. Romp: playful accumulation

### Visible sequence

- **0 to about 2 s:** desktop and phone presentations reframe into the desktop page. This is showcase movement, not evidence that the website zooms its own viewport.
- **4.8 to 6.4 s:** the preceding scalloped boundary leaves; a heading resolves, then the puppy, cat, grooming, and play cards appear in a short overlapping sequence. An underline develops while the later cards arrive; a small sticker follows. The heading becomes readable before the decoration is finished.
- **8.6 to 10.2 s:** a lilac product region arrives with a wavy edge and paw shapes. A large blue bag, lime support shape, and white selector form the main composition; three small evidence badges follow at different moments. Around **10.8 to 11.0 s**, a size selection and displayed price change together.
- **14 to 17 s:** care-list rows receive lime text/arrow emphasis near the pointer while the main photograph stays stable. Around **17.8 to 20 s**, a phone and heading establish a new scene, then a rating, message-like tiles, and drawn trails accumulate.
- **25.1 to 27.5 s:** invitation copy and dogs appear first; white curls continue afterward. At approximately **27.3 s**, the CTA has a rising dark fill and vertically displaced copies of its label inside a rounded clip. This is more specific than a generic color fade. The second CTA remains a quiet outline.
- **34.5 to 35.7 s:** the mobile category rail shifts from puppy toward cat, followed by ordinary vertical movement. The desktop mosaic has been recomposed into a rail.

### Interpretation and transferable decisions

The personality comes from a family of related contours: scallops, paws, soft cards, and hand-drawn curls. Motion repeats those relationships without making every element bounce. Main content establishes the scene; smaller accents provide follow-through. Uneven but coordinated arrival gives an assembled, playful quality. The calm selector and outlined button create useful visual contrast with the illustrated regions.

### Proposed construction and limits

Use separate layers for card arrival, image response, and decorative strokes. A path reveal can draw an original underline; a clipped overlay and visual label copies can reproduce the button relationship while retaining one semantic label and a stable hit area. Treat marketing message tiles as illustration unless genuine application events drive them. Never inherit the source's numerical claims, prices, testimonials, or implied health benefits as facts. On touch, expose category selection directly; in reduced motion, show a composed static scene with clear control feedback. Test pointer exit during the button fill and reversal halfway through the rail.

## C02. Vestmark: architectural continuity

### Visible sequence

- **0 to 2 s:** restrained copy appears above a large architectural photograph. The building supplies the dominant visual weight.
- **5.0 to 5.6 s:** a short image opening develops into an offset, rounded geometric aperture while clipped digits resolve into a large metric. Around **6.8 to 7.4 s**, another photo enters the same shape with a new metric; a further change around **8.3 to 9.2 s** ends with an occupancy percentage.
- **11.9 to 14.3 s:** a photographic band travels horizontally across fund rows near the pointer. At about **12.9 s**, the upper row is photographic while the next is partly revealed against blue. Titles, descriptions, figures, and arrow positions remain structurally anchored. This is a layered wipe, not a whole row sliding away.
- **25.3 to 27.5 s:** a media sequence enters; the strip travels left while the next image broadens and its neighbor narrows. The title and navigation retain their positions.
- The geometric mark returns as a large compositional motif later in the page and footer.

### Interpretation and transferable decisions

Repeated aperture geometry gives several otherwise different scenes a common identity. Straight travel and stable typographic columns echo the architecture. The mask is the continuity object while its photographic contents change. Restrained motion can still feel authored through crop, proportion, and exact layering.

### Proposed construction and limits

Keep the mask container and media layer independently controllable. Row previews can reveal an image under a fixed text layer; check contrast at partial coverage, not only at each endpoint. Separate the pointer target from moving media. The metric scenes have different meanings and units: update label, value, unit, and scope as one semantic group. Do not numerically interpolate fund size into occupancy or animate invented investment growth. The stable frame does not prove pinning, scroll scrubbing, or a specific library. A new version could use explicit steps, a time-based scene, or native-scroll progress according to the actual task.

## C03. FORMA: synchronized progression

### Visible sequence

- **0 to about 4.8 s:** a red photographic athlete cutout crosses enormous headline lettering. The captured pose is largely stable here; it is not evidence of a jumping character animation.
- **About 4.93 to 5.4 s:** the hero ghosts away as a timeline composition comes into view. The recording does not establish whether this is an interaction, a prototype transition, or an editing handoff.
- **6.5 to 10.5 s:** the week number advances from 01 toward 12, an arc grows, cards travel horizontally, selected content strengthens, and numerical annotations change. Week 05 is visible around **8.1 s**; week 12 is visible around **10.5 s**, followed by a hold.

### Interpretation and transferable decisions

Several signals describe one progression. The large week marker provides orientation while the moving cards supply narrative detail. This coherence is more consequential than any individual easing curve. The ring's stable center anchors the lateral sequence; changes of opacity distinguish emphasis without requiring every card to disappear.

### Proposed construction and limits

Drive the ring, current step, card position, and emphasis from one progress/selection model. Keep discrete content boundaries explicit; interpolate visual geometry where appropriate. Offer selectable steps and a readable static sequence. Do not turn the demonstration's week, weight, or exercise claims into a forecast for a real person. The source does not establish scrolling, an actual fitness app, or medical efficacy. For operational progress, the data determines the step; an animation never determines whether the work is complete.

## C04. Pedrix: staged product theatre

### Visible sequence

- **0 to 7 s:** an entire page sheet tilts and travels through perspective. The external background and sheet movement identify presentation-camera work, not a requirement for the live page.
- **8.6 to 10.7 s:** a large wordmark contracts toward the header. A thin media strip opens downward into a broad image area. The subject retains photographic proportions as the visible window grows.
- **About 11 s onward:** moving cyclist footage is present. Motion within footage must be distinguished from motion of the page's media box. Heading material is staged after the image around **12.2 to 13 s**.
- **20.7 to 23.5 s:** a circular illustration develops from center icon through segmented ring and changing percentage. It is presented as product storytelling; real battery telemetry is not established.
- **24.5 to 25.9 s:** three differently proportioned photographs open from narrow slits in an overlapping order. The images reveal through their windows, with captions subordinate to the image group.
- **About 33 to 35.6 s:** a small cluster of dashboard-like tiles expands into a larger arrangement; interior gauges progress after the overall group has established itself.

### Interpretation and transferable decisions

Large quiet areas make tightly staged details noticeable. The wordmark changes role from spectacle to navigation; thin apertures turn into useful media. Group movement precedes internal complexity, making the sequence feel assembled rather than independently tweened. This is controlled product theatre with more than one scale of rhythm.

### Proposed construction and limits

Reveal the media using clipping or a mask without stretching the photograph. Separate group layout movement from inner indicators so transforms have clear owners. Keep navigation and useful information available during any brand introduction; do not copy a compulsory preloader from a showcase. Use static or user-controlled footage where needed. A production dashboard should show actual values and pending states, not replay an invented climb from zero on every visit. Test slow media, skipped introduction, and a resize during the expanding cluster.

## C05. Tenners: typographic collage

### Visible sequence

- The opening includes presentation reframing. Around **2.6 to 3.2 s**, four photographic fragments arrive around an oversized central headline on cream. Light letter segments cross dark photographs while dark green lettering remains on the cream field. The composition holds afterward.
- **4.5 to 5.1 s:** portrait windows reveal in an overlapping sequence. One portrait is wider in the settled layout, but these frames do not establish a hover-expanding accordion.
- Around **7 to 8 s**, an orange highlighted row and overlapping circular photograph concentrate attention within the list.
- **8.65 to 9.4 s:** a photograph reveals before the adjacent quotation panel becomes fully visible; the composed pair holds until the next movement.
- A narrow striped boundary precedes the closing invitation around **11 to 12 s**.

### Interpretation and transferable decisions

The expressive unit is the relationship between lettering and photography. Four images converge around one typographic center rather than each receiving an unrelated parallax effect. Changing figure/ground relationships keep the collage connected. Staged media and quotation establish a reading order without making the whole page mechanically sequential.

### Proposed construction and limits

Prototype the image/type intersections early. An aligned duplicate text layer clipped to the image region is one possible construction; a blend mode is another, but it can alter color unpredictably. Neither is proven as the source technique. Preserve a single accessible heading, language shaping, and readable contrast at intermediate states. Recompose intersections for narrow screens instead of shrinking the desktop poster. Learning this sequence does not endorse every source component or override the owner's explicit defaults about announcement pills above hero titles.

## C06. forge: confident travel and quiet content

### Visible sequence

- **2.4 to about 3.5 s:** a pointer approaches the compact navigation, then the page travels to a statistics region and settles. A second travel occurs around **5.3 to 5.9 s**.
- **9.45 to 9.9 s:** the dark footer region enters. The compact navigation changes contrast/accent to stay distinct within the new field.
- The main content relies substantially on page movement and its strong settled composition. There is less independent element choreography than in Romp or Pedrix.
- Around **11.5 to 13 s**, the recording enlarges the footer CTA and pulls back. Treat that as presentation reframing unless additional product evidence establishes otherwise.

### Interpretation and transferable decisions

The motion experience comes from distance, destination, and a legible stop. Persistent navigation makes travel understandable. Yellow, black, large typography, and strong spacing carry the identity while the motion leaves much of the content quiet. This reference challenges the idea that an engaging site needs a reveal on every object.

### Proposed construction and limits

Try native anchor navigation with an appropriate motion preference, header offsets, and meaningful focus behavior. Make destination sections directly reachable. Change navigation contrast from reliable section state rather than a fragile guessed scroll number. The recording does not prove the click handler or a smooth-scroll library. Preserve browser history and normal wheel/touch input. Judge repeated navigation, not just the first journey.

## C07. Kinetic athlete poster: occlusion and rhythm

### Visible sequence

This is a graphic poster, not a demonstrated interface. A textured monochrome athlete sits with a vertical black band, a small red disk, and a larger partly hidden red circle. Between roughly **0.4 and 1.2 s**, the band moves across the composition; it holds near the right around **1.2 to 1.7 s**, then returns around **2.0 to 2.7 s**. The smaller accent and athlete maintain a coordinated relationship while the larger circle changes its visible portion. The ending approaches the opening arrangement.

### Interpretation and transferable decisions

Occlusion makes a few simple shapes feel spatial. A hold gives the composition time to register, and the return creates a phrase. The frozen pose supplies energy without needing character animation. Grain, black field, red accents, and controlled negative space do as much expressive work as the movement itself.

### Proposed construction and limits

Borrow the idea of a bounded kinetic illustration, offset phases, and alternating positive/negative space. Build original assets and layer relationships. Do not transfer the whole wipe onto operational navigation merely because it looks compelling. A three-second file is not proof of a seamless loop, and indefinite repetition lasts longer than three seconds. Inspect the loop boundary and apply relevant playback/pause and reduced-motion requirements. No soundtrack or synchronization was evaluated.

## C08. DentalProClinic: overlapping surface chapters

### Visible sequence

- A tablet mockup rotates and changes scale outside the page. That device-camera motion is separate from the displayed interface.
- **0.4 to 1.45 s:** a teal rounded surface enters beneath the preceding white region; heading, photograph, and support content resolve into a stable chapter. Overlapping edges and rounding maintain the identity of the surfaces.
- Around **3 to 5 s**, a large aligner photograph and service content establish another sequence.
- **5.7 to 8.3 s:** a portrait appears, a dark rounded plane enters, and heading, copy, and booking controls accumulate. The booking row becomes visible around **7.1 s**, with the final composition held later.

### Interpretation and transferable decisions

The page feels like successive material planes rather than disconnected boxes. Color and contour maintain continuity across chapters. Image-led staging gives each chapter a subject before secondary content fills it in. The calm result depends on the settled arrangement as much as on movement.

### Proposed construction and limits

Use overlapping section surfaces with reserved media space and a persistent navigation layer. Remove the mockup rotation when implementing the actual page unless separately justified. In a working appointment flow, fields must remain accessible with drafts, validation, and submission intact; do not require visitors to wait for every decorative layer before booking. The clip does not prove a native tablet app, functioning form, clinical claims, or production accessibility. Test focused fields while scrolling and ensure masks never hide validation or focus.

## C09. Cicileta: shutters and shared-image expansion

### Visible sequence

- **1.6 to 2.2 s:** a destination changes through vertical image slices moving in opposing directions. At **1.8 s**, the mountain photograph occupies most of the viewport while a central remnant of purple sky remains above and outer remnants remain below. The title passes through horizontal line masks. Navigation and peripheral controls largely retain their positions. A similar transition occurs around **3.6 to 4.2 s**.
- **7.5 to 9.9 s:** the hero's details withdraw, a sparse invitation bridges into a gallery, and the gallery becomes fully present. This establishes a pause in the recording's pace.
- The gallery shifts to center the purple Wanaka photograph. Around **13.8 to 14.3 s**, that image enlarges while surrounding tiles fade; the heading then returns, with a settled hero around **14.6 s**. The same recognizable subject connects the grid to the full image.

### Interpretation and transferable decisions

Counter-directional slices create tension while stable controls preserve orientation. The later expansion uses a different mechanism but the same image identity, producing variety within a coherent photographic experience. The quieter bridge gives visual breathing room. It need not become forced dead time in an interactive adaptation.

### Proposed construction and limits

Use a shared cover-crop coordinate system across masked slices so they form one photograph when settled. Independently applying `cover` to each narrow slice would produce mismatched crops. A clipped grid or aligned full-size layers can implement the idea; WebGL is not established or inherently required. Keep the semantic title whole even if visual copies reveal by line. For grid-to-hero continuity, retain the selected image's focal point while its rectangle changes and surrounding context recedes. Navigation, direct entry, Back, selection, and failure must work independently of the image transition. The clips do not establish autoplay versus manual advance or URL behavior. Verify sample copy independently before reuse.

## Relationships to carry forward

These are design hypotheses grounded in the observations above, not universal rules or a checklist of effects.

| Relationship | Evidence | New design question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main scene first, accent follow-through | C01, C04, C08 | Can the content be ready while decorative motion completes? |
| A persistent shape holds changing media | C02 | Which contour belongs to this identity, and what can change inside it? |
| Several signals share one progression | C03 | Which state should own the number, highlight, geometry, and content? |
| Image/type overlap is designed through time | C05 | Are figure/ground and readable text preserved at every important intersection? |
| Travel has a clear destination and hold | C06 | Does navigation settle where the next action is easy to recognize? |
| Occlusion and phase create a graphic phrase | C07 | Could a small, bounded illustration supply this character? |
| Surface geometry connects chapters | C01, C08 | Can the next section grow from the composition already present? |
| Selected media survives a change of role | C09, C04 | Which recognizable object should carry the transition? |
| Different mechanisms share one vocabulary | All, in different ways | What repeats: direction, contour, crop, tempo, material, or object identity? |

Do not make all new projects resemble these examples. Transfer a relationship, develop original art direction, and compare a real prototype. Preserve room for intuitive visual experimentation. Choose motion that is beautiful in the actual composition and dependable under real input, not merely easy to name.
