Brass Window: art direction and production handoff

Status: written proposal. Brass Window is a fictional VR company with no supplied identity. Assume the guide serves designers, developers, and production partners. Its offering, customers, positioning, and product appearance remain unknown. The identity below is proposed, not approved. Use English for this edition; confirm language needs before font selection.

The working idea is “A change of position changes what you see.” Build the visual language around parallax: a steady foreground opening reveals different relationships in the space beyond. This connects the name with VR’s spatial character without inventing hardware or a company history. Aim for tactile warmth, clear explanation, and moments of spatial surprise. The reader should notice the changed view, recognize Brass Window, then understand how to reproduce the system.

Before developing the guide, make three comparable roughs, each containing a cover, a dense rules page, and a small application. Route A, Near / Far, uses paired views of one scene with a shared foreground anchor. Route B, Field Notes, uses a continuous panoramic image with small observations arranged along its horizon. Route C, Inside the Word, uses large letter counters as image apertures, with typography controlling the page. Recommend A provisionally because changing viewpoint can organize images, page transitions, and applications. Choose only after viewing all three at equal fidelity; these alternatives have not been rendered.

For Route A, develop a custom wordmark from sturdy, open letterforms. Explore a restrained offset within the W while keeping the full name immediately readable. Build exact lettering in vectors. Test an accompanying mark made from two displaced openings, including whether it becomes a generic window icon. Let the wordmark carry the identity if the symbol adds little. Derive clear space from an identifiable letter feature and minimum sizes from actual reduction tests. Never distort the mark to imitate perspective.

Use chalk #F2EEE6 as the main field, deep blue-black #172C32 for reading, muted brass #B99A57 for small edges and chapter accents, and mist #B7CED0 for secondary fields. These are provisional sRGB values. Brass is a flat color, not a promise of metallic reproduction. Keep essential copy on calm fields; establish approved foreground/background pairs after measurement. Shortlist IBM Plex Sans for instructions and Source Serif 4 for large introductory statements. Compare them using the name, long headings, captions, numerals, and punctuation. Start body specimens at 12/17 pt with 55–70 characters per line; verify files, glyphs, licenses, and exported rendering before adoption.

Create one original scene family: a near opaque plane, an opening, and a distant curved structure under broad side light. Produce two views of the same geometry, with consistent materials and lighting. A controlled 3D scene is preferable where viewpoint continuity matters; generated imagery can explore atmosphere. Use opaque scenes, precise vector masks, and separate live typography. Show neither invented product screens nor fabricated headset photographs. Preserve spatial continuity, clean silhouettes, and intentional crops.

Plan a 16-page, 240 × 300 mm portrait guide, delivered digitally first. Pages 1–2 establish the idea and navigation; 3–5 explain identity variants and misuse; 6 covers color; 7 typography; 8–9 imagery; 10–11 layout; 12 motion principles; 13–15 applications; 16 a quick reference and asset index. Use a six-column grid, stable annotation margins, and alternating expansive image pages with compact instruction pages. Demonstrate a poster, a square announcement, and a dense partner document. Keep rules readable without facing-page dependence.

Produce editable layout files, vector identity masters, individual image assets, a bookmarked PDF, and an asset/license register. Motion remains a storyboard proposal: a fixed aperture, lateral viewpoint shift, and static alternative. Inspect every exported page, smallest logo use, font rendering, contrast, reading order, links, and image continuity. Record PASS, FAIL, or NOT RUN against specific versions. Visual, accessibility, and export checks are currently NOT RUN. Print specifications, physical proofs, trademark clearance, and audience validation remain outstanding.
